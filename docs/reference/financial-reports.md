# Financial reports

Paisa builds its reports from your journal and the prices available for its
commodities. Start with the question you want to answer:

| Question | Report |
| --- | --- |
| What do my asset accounts hold today? | **Assets → Balance** |
| How has my net worth changed? | **Assets → Net Worth** |
| How much did I invest over time? | **Assets → Investment** |
| Where did I spend money this month or year? | **Expenses → Monthly** or **Yearly** |
| How much income and tax did I record? | **Income** |
| What moved in and out of checking accounts? | **Cash Flow → Monthly** |
| How do annual flows compare by account? | **Cash Flow → Yearly** |
| What does the yearly profit-and-loss view show? | **Cash Flow → Income Statement** |

## Balances and net worth

**Asset Balance** displays the account hierarchy with market value, cost, and
performance where prices are available. **Net Worth** plots assets, liabilities,
and investment performance over a selected period.

Missing or stale prices can affect converted balances and performance figures.
Use [Commodities and prices](commodities.md) to configure price sources and
[Doctor](doctor.md) to find valuation problems.

## Income and expenses

Monthly and yearly expense reports group spending through the account
hierarchy. The Income report compares monthly income and investment activity,
and provides financial-year totals for gross income, net income, and tax.

These reports reflect the account structure and postings in your journal. If a
transaction is assigned to the wrong account, fix the journal or use
[Bulk edit](bulk-edit.md) where an account rename is appropriate.

## Investment activity

**Assets → Investment** shows how much money you invested over time, with
monthly and financial-year breakdowns. It measures contributions rather than
returns.

- **Investment** answers how much money went in.
- [Investment performance](investment-performance.md) answers how those
  investments performed.
- [Portfolio Analysis](analysis.md) shows what the investments contain.

## Cash flow and income statement

Monthly Cash Flow compares income, expenses, investments, tax, and checking
balances. Yearly Cash Flow shows annual movement at the selected account depth.
The Income Statement presents a yearly profit-and-loss view and a multi-year
account comparison.
